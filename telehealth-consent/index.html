Telehealth: how it works, and your consent

1. What telehealth is at this practice

A telehealth visit is a scheduled appointment with Dr. Mabini conducted by secure video, using Doxy.me — a HIPAA-compliant, encrypted platform that requires no app or account on your side. You join with one tap from your portal when the visit opens. It is a real visit: it is documented in your record, and it carries the same professional standards as an in-person appointment.

2. Where you must be

Medical licensure is state-by-state. You must be physically present in Illinois or California at the time of a telehealth visit — the states where Dr. Mabini currently holds active licenses. At the start of each visit you will be asked to confirm the address where you are, both for licensure and so help can be sent to the right place in an emergency. If you are anywhere else, the visit must be rescheduled; the preparation tools in the portal keep working wherever you are.

3. Benefits, limits, and risks

  • Benefits: access without travel or time off work, shorter waits, and continuity with the same physician who knows your history.
  • Limits: some things genuinely require hands and instruments — a pelvic examination, imaging, a procedure. Some visit types therefore require in-person attendance, and the scheduling system will say so. A video visit may end with the conclusion that an in-person visit is needed; that is the system working, not failing.
  • Risks: as with any technology, transmission can fail or degrade, and despite strong safeguards no electronic channel is perfectly secure. Rarely, the limits of video could delay recognition of something an in-person exam would catch — which is exactly why in-person follow-up is arranged whenever clinically indicated.
  • Alternatives: in-person visits are always available as an alternative, and choosing one over telehealth never affects the quality or priority of your care.

4. Privacy during a video visit

  • Visits take place over an encrypted connection, and your health information remains protected by our Notice of Privacy Practices.
  • We do not record visits. Neither audio nor video, ever, without your separate explicit consent. We ask the same of you: do not record a visit without discussing it first.
  • Take the visit somewhere you can speak privately. Anyone present in the room with you should be someone you want to hear the conversation; tell Dr. Mabini who is there.

5. Chaperones

Certain visit types — complex new-patient consultations that include pelvic-area history or any examination component — require a chaperone. For a telehealth visit this means an adult chaperone present in the room with you during any examination portion. The scheduling system tells you when this applies to your visit type, and confirming it is part of booking.

6. If the technology fails

If video drops or quality makes the visit unsafe to continue, the visit moves to the phone number on your account, and if that fails, we reschedule at no disadvantage to you. Note your visit's phone-backup expectation when you join. A failed connection never simply ends your visit without follow-up.

7. Cost

A telehealth visit is billed like an in-person visit: to your health plan under its telehealth benefits, with your plan's usual cost-sharing, or at the practice's posted rates if you are self-pay. Insurance coverage of telehealth is set by your plan and by state law, and Illinois and California both require broad parity; your plan can confirm the specifics of your policy.

8. Prescriptions by telehealth

Most medications can be prescribed through a telehealth visit exactly as in person. There is one legal exception: controlled substances are governed by additional federal and state rules, and prescribing them by telehealth is restricted — in many circumstances federal law requires an in-person evaluation first. If a controlled substance is part of your plan, Dr. Mabini will tell you what the law permits in your situation, which may mean an in-person visit. A telehealth visit is never a shortcut around those rules, and no prescription of any kind is guaranteed by booking a visit.

9. Who licenses and regulates this care

Dr. Mabini is a Doctor of Osteopathic Medicine, licensed as a physician and surgeon in Illinois and California. You always have the right to raise concerns with the licensing authority in your state, in addition to raising them with the practice:

  • Illinois: Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation (IDFPR) — idfpr.illinois.gov.
  • California (the notice prescribed by 16 CCR §1606):
    NOTICE TO CONSUMERS
    Osteopathic physicians and surgeons (D.O.) are licensed and regulated by the Osteopathic Medical Board of California.
    (916) 928‑8390
    To check the status of your physician and surgeon D.O. license online, go to https://search.dca.ca.gov/.
    To file a complaint against the physician and surgeon D.O., complete the online complaint form on the Osteopathic Medical Board of California website or email osteopathic@dca.ca.gov.

When you acknowledge this consent at booking, your signed-and-dated electronic acknowledgment also records that you received the notice above, as the regulation permits for new patients.

10. Your consent and your rights

When you book your first telehealth visit, the portal asks you to confirm that you have reviewed this page, and your confirmation is recorded — with the date and the version of this document — as the documentation of consent. Your consent is voluntary and revocable: you may decline or stop telehealth at any time, and choose in-person care instead, without any effect on your access to treatment. Consent to telehealth is documented in your record, as Illinois (225 ILCS 150) and California (Bus. & Prof. Code §2290.5) provide. Questions before you consent are welcome: info@mountzara.com.
