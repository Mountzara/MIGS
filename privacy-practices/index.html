1. Who this covers

This notice describes the privacy practices of Mount Zara, LLC and Christopher-Armand Zaragoza Mabini, DO, MSAEd ("the Practice"), for protected health information ("PHI") — information that identifies you and relates to your health, the care you receive, or payment for that care. It applies to care delivered in person and by telehealth, to the member portal, and to every record the Practice holds about you.

2. How we may use and disclose your health information

The law permits use and disclosure of PHI without your separate authorization for three core purposes:

Treatment

To provide and coordinate your care. Example: reviewing the history you entered in your intake before your visit, or sending your operative report to another clinician who is treating you — including the OB/GYN you keep, if you use our preparation tools.

Payment

To bill and collect payment for your care. Example: submitting a claim with diagnosis and procedure codes to your health plan, or confirming your coverage before a visit.

Health care operations

To run the Practice competently: quality review, audit, accreditation, and administration. Example: reviewing records internally to assess the quality of care.

Appointment reminders and care communications

We will contact you with appointment reminders and information about your care — for example, an email that a booking is confirmed, that a visit is ready to join, or that something is waiting in your portal. These messages deliberately contain no clinical detail. We may also tell you about treatment alternatives and health-related services relevant to your care. You can ask us to use a particular address or channel — see "Confidential communications" under Your Rights.

Incidental disclosures that occur despite reasonable safeguards — a name overheard in a clinic — are permitted by law. We apply the minimum necessary standard to uses and disclosures other than for treatment.

3. Other situations where disclosure is permitted or required

  • Required by law, and only to the extent the law requires.
  • Public health — disease reporting, FDA-regulated product safety, and similar activities required or authorized by law.
  • Abuse, neglect, or domestic violence — reporting as required or, in defined circumstances, permitted by law, with your agreement where the law allows us to seek it.
  • Health oversight — audits and inspections by agencies that oversee the health care system.
  • Judicial and administrative proceedings — in response to a court order, or a subpoena where the legal conditions protecting you have been met. See section 5 for the limits we apply.
  • Law enforcement — only in the narrow circumstances HIPAA permits, and see section 5.
  • Coroners, medical examiners, funeral directors; organ donation.
  • To avert a serious threat to your health or safety or another person's, disclosed only to someone able to help prevent it.
  • Workers' compensation, as authorized by those laws.
  • People involved in your care — with your agreement, or when you are unable to agree and it is in your best interest, we may share relevant information with a family member or other person you involve in your care. You can name, or exclude, specific people.

4. Uses that require your written authorization

We will not do any of the following without your signed authorization, which you may revoke at any time in writing (except to the extent we have already relied on it):

  • Marketing communications.
  • Any sale of your PHI — which, separately, we simply do not do.
  • Disclosure of psychotherapy notes, in the rare case any exist.
  • Any use or disclosure not described in this notice.

5. Reproductive health privacy

This is a gynecology practice, and we treat the sensitivity of reproductive health information as central, not incidental. As a matter of Practice policy, and consistent with the shield laws of Illinois and California protecting lawful reproductive health care:

  • We do not volunteer reproductive health information to any investigation, and we do not disclose it in response to legal process without the specific legal review the law entitles you to — a subpoena is not a court order, and we treat the difference seriously.
  • We do not use or disclose your information to assist any effort to investigate or penalize a person for seeking, obtaining, providing, or facilitating reproductive health care that was lawful where it was provided.
  • Where the law gives us discretion, we exercise it in favour of your privacy.

6. Information with extra legal protection

Some categories of health information carry protections beyond HIPAA under Illinois and California law, and where a state law is more protective than HIPAA, the more protective law controls. In this practice these categories genuinely arise:

  • Mental health information — including the brief screening questionnaires the portal administers — is protected in Illinois by the Mental Health and Developmental Disabilities Confidentiality Act, which restricts disclosure more tightly than HIPAA and generally requires your specific written consent.
  • HIV/AIDS and sexually transmitted infection information is protected by the Illinois AIDS Confidentiality Act and California Health & Safety Code provisions, each requiring specific consent for most disclosures.
  • Genetic information — including family-history risk assessment — is protected by the Illinois Genetic Information Privacy Act and federal law (GINA), which among other things prohibit disclosure to employers and restrict insurer use.
  • Substance use disorder treatment records, where they exist, carry federal protections under 42 CFR Part 2.

Where one of these applies, we obtain the specific consent the statute requires before disclosing, even where HIPAA alone would permit the disclosure.

7. Minors

Illinois and California each allow minors to consent to certain sensitive services — including contraception, pregnancy-related care, and STI diagnosis and treatment — without a parent or guardian, and where a minor lawfully consented to care, information about that care is confidential and generally may not be disclosed to a parent without the minor's permission. Where care is provided to a minor in this practice, we follow the consent and confidentiality rules of the state where the care is provided, and a parent or guardian's portal access, where granted, is limited accordingly.

8. Your rights

You have the right to:

  • Inspect and receive a copy of your records, including an electronic copy, generally within 30 days of a written request. We may charge a reasonable cost-based fee for copies. Much of your record is available immediately in your portal.
  • Request an amendment if you believe something is incorrect or incomplete. We may decline in defined circumstances, in writing, and you may add a statement of disagreement to your record.
  • An accounting of disclosures we have made of your PHI, other than for treatment, payment, operations, and certain excepted categories, for the six years before your request.
  • Request restrictions on how we use or disclose your PHI. We are not required to agree to most restrictions — but if you pay for a service in full out of pocket and ask us not to tell your health plan about it, we must and will honour that.
  • Confidential communications — ask us to contact you at a specific phone number, address, or only through the portal, and we will accommodate reasonable requests without asking why.
  • A paper copy of this notice on request, even though it is posted here.
  • Be notified of a breach of unsecured PHI affecting you.
  • Name a personal representative with legal authority to act on your behalf, who may exercise these rights for you.

To exercise any of these rights, write to info@mountzara.com.

9. Our duties

  • We are required by law to maintain the privacy and security of your PHI, to give you this notice of our legal duties and privacy practices, and to abide by the notice currently in effect.
  • We will tell you promptly if a breach occurs that may have compromised your information.
  • We may change this notice; changes apply to the PHI we already hold. The current version is always posted at this address with its effective date, and material changes will be flagged in the portal.

10. Complaints

If you believe your privacy rights have been violated, you may complain to us at info@mountzara.com or (872) 365‑3866, and to the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, Office for Civil Rights — 200 Independence Avenue S.W., Washington D.C. 20201, 1-877-696-6775, or hhs.gov/ocr/complaints. We will never retaliate against you for filing a complaint.
