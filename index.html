How the surgery is done

Small incisions, even for big operations

1,475 of 1,511 procedures performed through small incisions

Large fibroids, extensive scar tissue, advanced endometriosis — the operations that once meant a long open incision are done laparoscopically or robotically. That means less pain, a smaller scar, and a quicker return to your own life.

Your anatomy, seen before it is touched

90 dissections with the ureter identified by sight

The ureter — the fine tube that carries urine from your kidney to your bladder — lies close to where this surgery takes place. Opening the tissue around it and identifying it by sight is the surest way to protect it, and in this series that was done in 90 operations.

Outcomes tracked on published scales

444 cases scored on ClassIntra and Clavien–Dindo

ClassIntra and Clavien–Dindo are the scales the surgical community uses to grade outcomes from I to IV, so results from different practices can be compared like for like. Across this series 3.15% of cases had a reportable event, the great majority of them minor — the chart shows how they fell by grade. The full grade-by-grade record is published on the CV.

Home the same day

90.4% of minimally invasive cases go home the same day

Including patients who have already had abdominal or pelvic surgery. Small incisions, opioid-sparing anaesthesia and a planned recovery pathway mean you get to recover in the place you are most comfortable — your own home.

RPOC in an Arcuate Uterus: visually-directed hysteroscopic management

AAGL Global Congress · Surgical Video Library

Seeing the cavity directly, rather than working blind.

Retained products of conception in an arcuate uterus carry an outsized risk of incomplete evacuation and intrauterine synechiae when approached by blind D&C. Direct hysteroscopic visualization through a targeted endometrial sweep preserves anatomy and protects future fertility — captured here from a real case.

Watch the technique

A 43-year-old patient with severe abnormal uterine bleeding and chronic pelvic pain after endometrial ablation presented a diagnostic puzzle: a uterine isthmocele complicated by altered tissue architecture. The team used Indocyanine Green (ICG) fluorescence imaging to precisely localize the defect and guide minimally invasive repair. The case — paired with a comprehensive literature review — illustrates how ICG enhances surgical precision, shortens operative time, and reduces complications, marking a meaningful advancement in isthmocele care.

Using CT imaging from 194 patients stratified by WHO BMI categories, this study reveals that the deep epigastric vessels shift laterally with rising BMI — meaning classic "safe zone" landmarks for laparoscopic port placement become unreliable in obese patients. The recommendation: position trocars more than 10 cm from the midline when BMI exceeds 35. The findings directly impact surgical safety in a growing patient population.

View publication →

Adenomyosis remains a clinical chameleon — easy to suspect, harder to confirm. This retrospective cohort study evaluates Narrow Band Imaging (NBI) hysteroscopy as a real-time, in-office diagnostic enhancement. By emphasizing surface vascular patterns invisible to standard white light, NBI helps distinguish adenomyotic features from healthy tissue.

View publication →

An ongoing randomized controlled trial investigating whether transversus abdominis plane (TAP) blocks — regional anesthesia infiltrated into the abdominal-wall plane — meaningfully reduce post-operative pain scores and opioid use in minimally invasive gynecologic surgery when layered into an Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) protocol. Designed and conducted within the CBG/MIGS fellowship at PRIME Illinois St. Francis.

An infertility workup uncovered an unusually complex multifocal endometriosis — with atypical papillary fragments involving both the appendix and fallopian tube. This accepted manuscript documents the diagnostic pathway, surgical findings, and pathology, expanding the clinical understanding of how aggressively endometriosis can manifest beyond classical pelvic sites.
