The Techniques

Hands-on, evidence-aligned modalities.

Trained across the four core osteopathic manipulative techniques — each chosen and combined for the patient in front of me, not by protocol.

FDM

Fascial Distortion Model

A precise diagnostic and treatment framework targeting six specific fascial distortion patterns — particularly powerful for acute pain, restricted range of motion, and post-surgical adhesions.

ME

Muscle Energy

A direct, active technique using the patient's own muscle contractions against precise counterforce — restoring sacral mechanics, pelvic alignment, and lumbar function safely in pregnancy.

CS

Counterstrain

An indirect, gentle positional release method ideal for tender points, post-operative bodies, and patients in too much pain for direct techniques — calming the nervous system as it works.

MFR

Myofascial Release

Sustained pressure into restrictive fascial patterns — addressing scar tissue, chronic pelvic floor tension, abdominal wall restrictions, and the visceral-somatic dysfunctions central to pelvic pain.

Mount Zara Suite

Five apps. One mission.

A native suite for the modern physician — clinical decision support, board prep, research, transcription, and surgical media. Built end-to-end with modern AI. All apps are fully developed and currently in active beta testing.

Base AI runs on-device. Every app ships with native, on-device AI reasoning — fast, free, fully private. No internet required for the core workflow.
Optional Claude API for elevated reasoning. For users who want deeper synthesis or larger-context reasoning, every app supports bring-your-own Claude API key — toggleable per query.
Auto PHI redaction — on-device, before anything leaves. Every app auto-redacts patient identifiers (names, MRNs, DOBs, addresses, dates) on-device before any external call. PHI isn't even stored long-term — it's stripped from the in-memory pipeline. No PHI ever leaves the phone or the Mac.
HIPAA-compliant by design. Every app implements the security architecture documented in Medical Transcription: on-device de-identification, encrypted at rest, no cloud audio, no cloud transcripts, audit-logged access. The same security model applies across the suite.

To See or Not to See: a visually directed approach to RPOC in an arcuate uterus.

Journal of Minimally Invasive Gynecology · Vol. 31, Issue 11, S14 · AAGL Global Congress 2024

Why this case mattered

Retained products of conception in an arcuate uterus classically defy blind dilation and curettage. By substituting direct hysteroscopic visualization for blind instrumentation, the team preserved endometrial integrity and protected future fertility — establishing a new teaching standard recognized by AAGL with the Golden Hysteroscope Award.

Journal of Minimally Invasive Gynecology · Vol. 31, Issue 11, S58 · AAGL Global Congress 2024

Robotic myomectomy frequently bottlenecks at fibroid extraction. This work introduces a streamlined "à la carte" technique using a gel-based port and bag containment system — reducing total operative time, simplifying multi-fibroid removal, and preserving contained-morcellation safety.

When in Doubt, Green It Out: ICG-guided isthmocele excision in Asherman's syndrome.

Journal of Minimally Invasive Gynecology · Vol. 31, Issue 11, S10–S11 · AAGL Global Congress 2024

Mapping a hidden defect

Asherman's syndrome distorts uterine anatomy, complicating the already-difficult repair of an isthmocele. A combined hysteroscopic-robotic approach with indocyanine green (ICG) fluorescence identifies the defect's true margins despite scarred terrain — letting the surgeon excise precisely where blind landmarks fail.

AI Snapshot

A 43-year-old patient with severe abnormal uterine bleeding and chronic pelvic pain after endometrial ablation presented a diagnostic puzzle: a uterine isthmocele complicated by altered tissue architecture. The team used Indocyanine Green (ICG) fluorescence imaging to precisely localize the defect and guide minimally invasive repair. The case — paired with a comprehensive literature review — illustrates how ICG enhances surgical precision, shortens operative time, and reduces complications, marking a meaningful advancement in isthmocele care.

AI Snapshot

Using CT imaging from 194 patients stratified by WHO BMI categories, this study reveals that the deep epigastric vessels shift laterally with rising BMI — meaning classic "safe zone" landmarks for laparoscopic port placement become unreliable in obese patients. The recommendation: position trocars more than 10 cm from the midline when BMI exceeds 35. The findings directly impact surgical safety in a growing patient population.
