How the surgery is done

Small incisions, even for big operations

The complex operations that once meant a long open incision, done through a few small ones

Large fibroids, extensive scar tissue, advanced endometriosis — the operations that once meant a long open incision are done laparoscopically or robotically. That means less pain, a smaller scar, and a quicker return to your own life.

Your anatomy, seen before it is touched

The time taken to see and protect the delicate structures around the surgery

The ureter — the fine tube that carries urine from your kidney to your bladder — lies close to where this surgery takes place. Opening the tissue around it and identifying it by sight, rather than assuming where it runs, is the surest way to keep it safe — and that step is standard in every case, not saved for when trouble is expected.

Outcomes tracked on published scales

Results measured honestly against the standards surgeons share worldwide

ClassIntra and Clavien–Dindo are the scales the surgical community uses to grade outcomes from I to IV, so results from different practices can be compared like for like. A little over 3% of procedures had a reportable event, the great majority of them minor — the chart shows how they fell by grade. The full breakdown is published on the CV, so you can read the record rather than take anyone’s word for it.

Home the same day

Most patients recover at home the same day — 90.4% of minimally invasive cases

Including patients who have already had abdominal or pelvic surgery. Small incisions, opioid-sparing anaesthesia and a planned recovery pathway mean you get to recover in the place you are most comfortable — your own home.

Seven clinical practice areas
Every stage
Complete excision of endometriosis from superficial disease to deep infiltrating and bowel, bladder or ureteral involvement.

What this looks like in practice

Complete excision — not ablation — of superficial, deep infiltrating, and atypical lesions across all four anatomic compartments. ICG fluorescence used intraoperatively to illuminate fibrosis and inflammatory changes invisible under standard light, an active IRB-approved research methodology adopted from the fellowship.

  • Stages I through IV including bowel, bladder, ureter, and appendiceal involvement
  • Indocyanine green (ICG) guided excision for fibrosis detection
  • Multi-quadrant resection with anatomical sparing where disease permits
98%
of myomectomies completed with the uterus preserved — keeping fertility and anatomy intact wherever the disease allows.

What this looks like in practice

Robotic and laparoscopic myomectomy for fibroids of any size, number, or location — intramural, submucosal, subserosal, pedunculated. The gel-port containment system for tissue extraction is an AAGL-presented technique with the methodology published in JMIG.

  • Extended robotic cases for high fibroid burden
  • Gel-based port for contained tissue extraction
  • Uterine-sparing where future fertility is a priority
93%
of hysterectomies done through minimally invasive routes, including large and complex cases — less pain and a faster recovery.

What this looks like in practice

Every minimally invasive route — robotic, conventional laparoscopic, vNOTES, and where indicated hysteroscopic and cystoscopic adjuncts. Even large or complex cases are approached through small incisions wherever it is safe, so recovery happens at home; an open approach is chosen in advance only when the anatomy calls for it.

  • Robotic Da Vinci across the full complexity range
  • Conventional and reduced-port laparoscopy
  • vNOTES — vaginal natural orifice transluminal endoscopic surgery
  • Same-day discharge as the default expectation, not the exception
2024
AAGL Golden Hysteroscope Award — recognized for technique innovation.

What this looks like in practice

Operative hysteroscopy for myomectomy, polypectomy, septum resection, RPOC removal, isthmocele repair, lysis of intrauterine adhesions (Asherman’s), and endometrial ablation. Complemented by office hysteroscopy via the Bettocchi no-touch vaginoscopy technique — awake, atraumatic, no speculum or tenaculum.

  • Hysteroscopic myomectomy and septoplasty
  • Retained products of conception (RPOC) management without curettage
  • Isthmocele repair
  • In-office diagnostic and operative procedures outside the OR
Ovary spared
Ovarian cysts removed while protecting healthy ovarian tissue and future fertility whenever it is safe to do so.

What this looks like in practice

Surgical planning starts from the question of future fertility, not the other way around. Uterine-sparing approaches to fibroid disease, careful adhesion prevention, fallopian tube preservation where oncologic and anatomic considerations permit, isthmocele repair to optimize implantation, and reserve-conscious ovarian cystectomy with meticulous capsule dissection.

  • Uterine-sparing fibroid management when fertility is a goal
  • Reserve-conscious cystectomy — minimized cautery, judicious bipolar use
  • Adhesion prevention strategies built into the operative plan
  • Isthmocele repair for implantation-window optimization
Your own tissue
Native-tissue pelvic-floor repair matched to your anatomy, without mesh where the anatomy supports it.

What this looks like in practice

Uterosacral ligament suspension, anterior and posterior colporrhaphy, and compartment-specific apical support. Native-tissue repair as the default when anatomy permits, with graft augmentation reserved for the indications where the literature genuinely supports it.

  • Uterosacral ligament suspension for apical prolapse
  • Anterior and posterior colporrhaphy for site-specific defects
Seen, not assumed
Extensive dissection through scar tissue and across organ boundaries with the ureter and bowel identified by sight and protected.

What this looks like in practice

Multi-quadrant adhesive disease and dense bowel and pelvic-sidewall pathology, dissected carefully with the bowel and ureter protected as a matter of routine. Cross-specialty skill is layered on top: cystoscopy, ureteral stent placement, small-bowel repair, appendectomy and retroperitoneal dissection.

  • Multi-quadrant sharp and energy-based dissection
  • Cystoscopy and ureteral stent placement
  • Small bowel repair, appendectomy, retroperitoneal dissection

Golden Hysteroscope Award 2024

To See or Not to See: a visually directed approach to RPOC in an arcuate uterus

Journal of Minimally Invasive Gynecology · Vol. 31, Issue 11, S14 · AAGL Global Congress 2024

Direct hysteroscopic vision in place of blind curettage.

Retained products of conception in an arcuate uterus classically defy blind dilation and curettage. By substituting direct hysteroscopic visualization for blind instrumentation, the team preserved endometrial integrity and protected future fertility.

Seeing the cavity directly, rather than working blind.
