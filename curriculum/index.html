Fellowships, residencies, and clinical training programs are best served by curriculum that is designed, not assembled — grounded in evidence-based instructional design, anchored to ACGME and accreditation requirements, and built around the way adult learners actually learn. Methodology

A curriculum is a designed system.

Every program below was built using the same end-to-end instructional-design cycle. Each phase produces a deliverable. None of the phases are optional. Skipping context analysis or assessment design is how curricula drift into "we've always done it that way" by year three.

1 Context Analysis Institutional resources, learner profile, patient & community needs
2 Needs Assessment Community + fellow needs, ACGME gaps, equity priorities
3 Goals & Objectives Mission, competency-aligned objectives, measurable outcomes
4 Instructional Design Didactics, simulation, case-based learning, reflective practice
5 Assessment Formative + summative + competency milestones + case logs
6 Evaluation Program-level evaluation, continuous improvement, feedback loops
Andragogy

Malcolm Knowles

Adult Learning Theory: self-direction, prior experience, readiness to learn, problem-centered orientation, intrinsic motivation. The foundation for every adult-focused training program.

Experiential

David Kolb

Experiential Learning Cycle: concrete experience → reflective observation → abstract conceptualization → active experimentation. Especially powerful for surgical and procedural training.

Instructional events

Robert Gagné

Nine Events of Instruction: gain attention, inform objectives, recall prior knowledge, present content, provide guidance, elicit performance, give feedback, assess, enhance retention and transfer.

Systematic design

Dick & Carey

Systems-approach model: instructional goals, learner/context analysis, performance objectives, criterion-referenced assessment, instructional strategy, materials, formative + summative evaluation.

Compliance backbone

ACGME Core Competencies

Six competencies (Medical Knowledge, Patient Care, PBLI, ICS, Professionalism, Systems-Based Practice) mapped to milestones — the compliance and assessment backbone for every accredited program.

Transfer to practice

Transfer of Learning

Identifying and removing barriers to translating training into independent practice — institutional, cognitive, motivational. The piece most curricula skip and most graduates feel.

Consulting Services

What an institution can engage me to build.

Whether starting a new fellowship from scratch, refreshing a stalled program, or auditing an existing curriculum against current accreditation standards — these are the deliverables I produce. Each engagement is tailored to your institution.

Assess

Needs assessment

Community-level, patient-level, learner-level, and faculty-level needs assessment. Surveys, focus groups, gap analysis, equity audits. The foundation every other deliverable rests on.

Context analysis & stakeholder mapping

Institutional resources, faculty expertise, technology inventory, patient population, community partnerships, accreditation context, regulatory alignment. Mapped against your specific program model.

Curriculum audit & refresh

Diagnostic audit of an existing program: alignment to current ACGME milestones, completeness of context analysis, assessment validity, faculty satisfaction, learner outcomes. Refresh recommendations with phased implementation.

Design

Competency-based curriculum architecture

ACGME / AAGL / ABOG / society-aligned competency milestones, learning objectives mapped to ACGME six core competencies, multi-year rotation structure across one or many sites.

Instructional design

Didactic curricula, simulation integration, case-based learning, structured surgical-video review, scientific writing modules, journal clubs, conference frameworks, deliberate-practice protocols.

Multi-site rotation design

Coordinated curricula across multiple hospital sites with different patient populations, faculty profiles, and surgical case volumes. Same competencies, calibrated experience per site.

Equity-anchored design

Curricula built with health equity, social determinants of health, and community needs at the center of the design. The measure of success is outcomes that close gaps.

AI & advanced-technology integration

Embedding AI tools, surgical-intelligence platforms, video-analysis workflows, and simulation technology into one coherent learning experience. Every tool earns a defined instructional role.

Evaluate

Assessment design

Formative and summative assessment frameworks, milestone-based evaluation, OSCEs, simulation-based assessment, case log validation, 360° evaluation, narrative feedback structures.

Program evaluation

Program-level evaluation and continuous improvement frameworks — Kirkpatrick four levels, Stufflebeam CIPP, AAMC outcome metrics. Feedback loops that actually inform iteration.

Sustain

Faculty development

Faculty-as-educator training, mentorship structures, "teaching the teacher" workshops, evaluation calibration, feedback skill-building. Sustains the curriculum after launch.

Transfer-of-learning strategy

Identifying and removing barriers that block graduates from translating fellowship training into independent practice — institutional, cognitive, motivational, structural.
