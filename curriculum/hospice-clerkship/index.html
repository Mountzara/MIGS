Hospice Pilot Rotation Curriculum.

Designed and launched the first hospice and palliative care rotation at the Alabama College of Osteopathic Medicine. Built on Adult Learning Theory, Kolb's experiential learning cycle, and reflective practice. End-of-life care, family communication, symptom management — taught the way adult learners actually internalize hard material.

Christopher Z. Mabini, DO, MSAEd · Alabama College of Osteopathic Medicine, Dothan AL · Pilot launched 2018

Provenance

This rotation was designed and delivered by Dr. Mabini at the Alabama College of Osteopathic Medicine. The original design document lives in the institutional archive, and the complete end-to-end methodology is documented in the CBG/MIGS Fellowship case study. Theoretical Anchors

What this rotation was designed around.

Three frameworks shaped every decision in the design: who the learners were, how they would internalize the material, and what evidence of learning would count.

Andragogy

Knowles' Adult Learning Theory

Adult learners need self-direction, integration of prior experience, and a problem-centered orientation. The rotation put medical students in charge of their own engagement with end-of-life cases.

Experiential cycle

Kolb's Experiential Learning

The rotation was built to run Kolb's complete cycle:

  • Concrete experience: hospice care with patients and families
  • Reflective observation: structured debrief
  • Abstract conceptualization: didactic synthesis
  • Active experimentation: the next family meeting, the next symptom-management decision
Reflective practice

Narrative medicine + Schön

Reflection-in-action and reflection-on-action are the engine of competency growth in hard emotional territory. The rotation built in structured reflective journaling, narrative-medicine writing, and faculty-facilitated debriefs. Students wrote after emotionally significant encounters and revisited those entries with faculty.

Components

What the rotation actually included.

This was the first hospice and palliative care rotation at ACOM. Landing it took multi-stakeholder work with ACOM administration, Community Hospice in Dothan, and student affairs; Dr. Mabini designed, launched, and led the inaugural offering.

Clinical immersion

Direct patient and family engagement in the hospice setting under faculty supervision. Real cases, real conversations, real symptom-management decisions.

Structured debriefing

Faculty-facilitated debrief sessions after emotionally significant encounters. Standardized debrief protocol to support reflective-observation phase of Kolb cycle.

Narrative medicine

Reflective writing components grounded in the narrative-medicine tradition. Building the language for what cannot easily be said in clinical shorthand.

Symptom-management didactics

Pain control, dyspnea management, nausea, terminal restlessness, secretions. Evidence-based pharmacologic and non-pharmacologic approaches.

Family communication training

Goals-of-care conversations, breaking bad news, navigating disagreement among family members, code-status discussions. Skill-based, not lecture-based.

Interdisciplinary rounds

Participation in IDT rounds — physicians, nurses, social work, chaplaincy, bereavement. Systems-based-practice exposure beyond the medical-only frame.

Competency-based assessment

Faculty assessment against defined hospice/palliative-care competencies, plus self-assessment, plus reflective-journal review. Each student's evaluation was triangulated across all three sources.
