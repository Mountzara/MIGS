Executive Synthesis

Why this curriculum is different.

Every accredited FMIGS program teaches the same surgical content. What sets this one apart is the design discipline behind it — and that discipline is what an institution actually engages a curriculum-design consultant for.

The MountZara FMIGS curriculum trains surgeons in Complex Benign Gynecology and Minimally Invasive Gynecologic Surgery across a three-year fellowship at PRIME Illinois Saint Francis Hospital — extended from two years to a thirty-six-month, competency-based program — with clinical rotations spanning four hospital sites in the Chicago metro area. It is the curriculum that trained the inaugural fellow in this program — and it was architected, written, and operationalized before that fellow walked in the door.

What sets it apart isn't the surgical content; every accredited FMIGS program teaches those procedures. It is the design discipline behind the content — a formal context analysis of the institution and the community it serves, a needs assessment that names health disparities in CBG/MIGS access explicitly, instructional design grounded in Knowles's adult learning theory and Kolb's experiential cycle, thirty-six months of monthly didactic structure with paired reading lists and simulation training, and assessment systematically mapped to the ACGME six core competencies. The third year consolidates and extends Years 1–2, with emphasis on independent surgical practice, teaching leadership, and transition to autonomous MIGS practice.

For an institution considering a new fellowship — or refreshing one that has drifted into "we've always done it this way" by year three — this is the level of architecture an end-to-end curriculum-design engagement produces. The Didactic Spine

Stakeholder Architecture

Eight stakeholder groups, mapped explicitly.

Most curricula treat stakeholders as an afterthought. This one names eight groups, defines what each needs from the program and what each gives to it, and uses that map to guide every subsequent design decision.

Primary beneficiary

Fellows

The fellow seeking advanced CBG/MIGS training. The curriculum exists for their skill acquisition and clinical-reasoning development.

Educators

Faculty & mentors

Deliver instruction, supervise procedures, calibrate assessment. Quality of mentorship is the largest predictor of fellowship outcomes.

Care recipients

Patients

Receive improved access to advanced surgical care. Underserved populations explicitly named in the needs assessment.

Outreach

Community organizations

Partner in addressing health disparities through CBG/MIGS access. Curriculum integrates community health needs into clinical exposure.

Compliance

Accreditation bodies

ACGME + AAGL FMIGS Board. Curriculum is aligned to their published requirements and competencies.

Sponsor

Healthcare institution

Four hospital sites (SFH, SJH, SMH, TMH) provide infrastructure, faculty time, and patient volume.

Peer learning

Residents & medical students

Benefit from teaching interactions with the fellow. The fellow simultaneously develops their own teaching skill.

Influence

Policy makers & advocates

Healthcare-policy stakeholders interested in reducing CBG/MIGS-access disparities. Curriculum produces evidence-of-impact for that goal.

ACGME-Aligned Competencies

Six core competencies, mapped to milestones.

Every learning activity in the program is mapped to one or more of ACGME's six core competencies, and every competency is mapped to specific milestones the fellow advances through. Without this mapping, "do more cases" replaces deliberate competency development.

1

Medical Knowledge

MK

Anatomy, physiology, surgical principles, energy sources, complications. Driven by the monthly didactic spine, assigned readings, and literature analysis.

2

Patient Care

PC

Clinical decision-making, surgical skill, perioperative management. Built through graduated autonomy across four hospital sites.

3

Practice-Based Learning & Improvement

PBLI

Self-directed learning, evidence appraisal, deliberate practice. Reinforced through journal club, surgical video review, and the scientific writing module.

4

Interpersonal & Communication Skills

ICS

Patient communication, family meetings, interdisciplinary team collaboration. Practiced in clinic, OR, and tumor-board settings.

5

Professionalism

PROF

Ethical reasoning, equity-anchored care, professional growth. Surfaces explicitly in Y2 July module and in continuous reflective practice.

6

Systems-Based Practice

SBP

Multi-site healthcare-systems navigation, cost-conscious care, community-resource integration. Built through the four-hospital rotation design.

Four-Site Clinical Architecture

One fellow, four hospitals, calibrated case mix.

Each site is selected for what it teaches uniquely. The same competencies are pursued at every site; the case mix and patient population vary deliberately to build breadth.

Graduate Profile

By graduation, the fellow can —

The outcomes the curriculum was designed to produce. These are the questions the assessment package (chapter 16) and graduation requirements (chapter 15) are built to answer about every fellow before they leave the program.

Independently manage complex benign gynecologic pathology across the full surgical spectrum (laparoscopic, robotic, vaginal, hysteroscopic).
Lead an interdisciplinary CBG/MIGS team through pre-, intra-, and post-operative phases of care with confidence and clinical judgment.
Apply evidence-based decision-making at the point of care, including critical appraisal of new literature and emerging technology.
Recognize, manage, and prevent the full range of CBG/MIGS surgical complications — vascular, urologic, bowel, infectious, neuropathic.
Address health-equity gaps in CBG/MIGS access through community-aware practice and culturally-competent communication.
Teach the next generation — residents, students, peers — through faculty-development practice woven into the fellowship.
Conduct scholarly work — design, execute, present, and publish research; contribute to CBG/MIGS literature; participate in QI.
Navigate complex healthcare systems with cost-conscious, value-based decision-making and systems-based practice fluency.
