Hospice Care Training Program.

Comprehensive training curriculum for hospice care providers — symptom management, family-centered communication, ethical decision-making, team-based interdisciplinary rounds. Built on andragogy and transformative learning frameworks, designed for adult healthcare professionals transitioning into palliative care.

Christopher Z. Mabini, DO, MSAEd · Community Hospice, Dothan AL

Theoretical Anchors

Designed for experienced clinicians entering end-of-life care.

Clinicians moving into hospice and palliative care are not novice learners. They bring full clinical identities — and have to undergo a real shift in how they relate to medicine, families, and the meaning of care. The framework choices reflect that.

Andragogy

Knowles' Adult Learning Theory

Self-direction, prior-experience integration, problem-centered orientation. Clinicians enter with deep clinical knowledge and specific gaps in end-of-life work, so the program was built around the experience they already carried.

Transformative

Mezirow's Transformative Learning

Disorienting dilemmas → critical reflection → discourse → action. Hospice work confronts clinicians with assumptions about cure, autonomy, and the meaning of "doing something" — transformative learning theory is purpose-built for that shift.

Reflective practice

Schön's Reflective Practitioner

Reflection-in-action and reflection-on-action embedded throughout. Structured case discussions and mentored debrief replace the "see one, do one" pattern that fails in palliative settings.

Components

What the training included.

Symptom management

Pain, dyspnea, nausea, terminal restlessness, secretions, anxiety. Evidence-based pharmacologic and non-pharmacologic approaches across cancer and non-cancer trajectories.

Family-centered communication

Goals-of-care conversations, breaking bad news, navigating family disagreement, code-status discussions, prognostication. SPIKES, NURSE, and Ask-Tell-Ask frameworks practiced, not just taught.

Ethical decision-making

Autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice in end-of-life care. Surrogate decision-making, MOLST/POLST, withdrawal of life-sustaining therapy, principle of double effect, futility.

Interdisciplinary team-based rounds

IDT rounds with physicians, nursing, social work, chaplaincy, bereavement, volunteer coordinators. Systems-based-practice training that reflects how hospice actually delivers care.

Simulated family meetings

Standardized-patient encounters with trained actors playing family members. Real-time faculty observation, structured debrief, repeated cycles with progressively complex scenarios.

Reflective case discussions

Faculty-facilitated case discussions centered on emotionally significant patients. Builds the language and the inner habit of reflection-on-action.

Mentored clinical experiences

Direct supervision, graduated autonomy, structured feedback. Clinicians build hands-on palliative skill one supervised case at a time.

Self-care + sustainability

Compassion fatigue, moral distress, burnout prevention. The program treats clinician wellness as a core component, because sustained hospice work depends on it.
