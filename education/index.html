How these guides are written

Every claim carries its evidence, and you can read the original.

Each guide is built the same way. A clinical claim gets a numbered citation the moment it is made; hover or tap the number to see the paper’s title, journal, year and a plain-language summary of what it found; the full reference list at the bottom of the page holds every paper’s verbatim PubMed abstract, so you are never asked to take a summary on trust.

Cited inline, not in a footnote pile

Every figure — a percentage, a number of years, a response rate — points at the paper it came from, and that paper’s abstract states the figure.

Plain language, honest about uncertainty

Clinical terms are explained where they first appear. Where the evidence is thin or mixed, the guide says so rather than rounding it up.

No amounts to take

These pages describe options and how they are weighed. They never tell you how much of anything to take — that is a conversation for your visit, with your own history in front of you.

The surgical care described in these guides is complex benign gynecology and minimally invasive gynecologic surgery (CBG/MIGS), layered with osteopathic care where it helps. If something here does not match what you were told, that is worth a conversation — message the practice and ask.

This page is provided strictly for educational purposes and is not medical advice. Treatment decisions should be made with your own physician, based on your individual evaluation. If you spot an error, please email info@mountzara.com.
