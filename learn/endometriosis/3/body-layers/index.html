The osteopathic layer — the published trials

This is a DO-led CBG/MIGS practice — a fully licensed gynecologic surgeon with additional training in osteopathic manipulative treatment. For pelvic pain, OMT addresses the structural and neurological contributors that hormones and surgery don’t directly reach, layered on top of standard care rather than instead of it.

  • Muñoz-Gómez 2023 — RCT, n=41 · 8-week OMT protocol vs usual care in endometriosis-related pelvic pain. Significant improvements in pain and EHP-30 quality of life sustained at one-month follow-up[8]Effectiveness of a Manual Therapy Protocol in Women with Pelvic Pain Due to Endometriosis: A Randomized Clinical Trial.J Clin Med 2023 · PMID 37176750Randomized trial of 41 women with endometriosis-related pelvic pain: 8 weeks of hands-on manual (physical) therapy reduced pain intensity - a benefit still present at six months - and improved physical quality of life, supporting it as a complement to gynecologic care..
  • Molins-Cubero 2014 — Double-blind RCT, n=40 · A single sacroiliac manipulation produced immediate pain reduction with measurable increase in circulating serotonin and catecholamines[9]Changes in pain perception after pelvis manipulation in women with primary dysmenorrhea: a randomized controlled trial.Pain Med 2014 · PMID 24666560Randomized sham-controlled trial of 40 women with primary period pain: a single pelvic manipulation session briefly reduced self-reported low-back/pelvic pain and pressure sensitivity versus sham — a small study showing only short-term effects..
  • Ruffini 2018 — RCT, n=31 · Five OMT sessions reduced average menstrual pain by 63% (5.4 → 2.0/10); SF-12 physical +58%, mental +36%. FitzGerald 2012 — the NIH multicenter pelvic-floor PT RCT

Level I evidence from 11 NIH/NIDDK research centers that a specialized internal pelvic-floor myofascial physical therapy is substantively more effective than general massage in pelvic-floor pain — which is why the “PT” you receive matters enormously.
