Module 2 of 6 · Getting Diagnosed: Why It Takes So Long

You don't need surgery to be diagnosed — and where surgery still fits

Somewhere along the way you absorbed the idea that nothing counts until a surgeon has seen it — that your account of your own body is provisional until an operation says otherwise.

You do not need surgery to be diagnosed

For decades, a definitive diagnosis required laparoscopic surgery. That standard has changed. “Do I need surgery to know if I have endometriosis?”

No. ACOG[1]Diagnosis of Endometriosis.Obstet Gynecol 2026 · PMID 41712950ACOG Clinical Practice Guideline — Diagnosis of Endometriosis. Affirms clinical diagnosis as sufficient: laparoscopy is no longer required to start treatment. and ESHRE[13]ESHRE guideline: endometriosis.Hum Reprod Open 2022 · PMID 35350465ESHRE Endometriosis Guideline 2022 — European multidisciplinary consensus on diagnosis, pain management, infertility, and surgery. both now recommend that diagnosis can be made clinically — based on the symptom pattern, examination, and imaging — without requiring laparoscopy first. Diagnostic surgery still has a role for women who fail medical therapy, or when surgery would be the treatment anyway. But it is not the entry point.

Where surgery still fits

The national guideline is clear: definitive diagnosis only comes from histology of lesions removed at surgery. Imaging and serum markers have not been able to replace diagnostic laparoscopy. Transvaginal ultrasound is the imaging of choice (Level A) and helpful for endometriomas and DIE; pelvic MRI adds detail for deep disease.
