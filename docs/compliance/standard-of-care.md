## Section 1 — Statement of principle

Mount Zara provides the same standard of care to every patient, regardless of whether the visit is conducted in person or via telehealth. The legal standard, established by *Texas v. Teladoc* and reinforced by the Krystine White / Dr. Harris (2006) precedent, is that telehealth visits create the same physician-patient relationship as in-person visits, with the same obligations of care. Mount Zara accepts this standard as the operating baseline for the practice.

## Section 2 — Definition of standard of care

The standard of care for any clinical encounter is what a reasonable practitioner of the same training and experience would do under similar circumstances. For Mount Zara, this means that a CBG/MIGS surgeon's standard of care applies — not a general gynecologist's standard, and not a general practitioner's. The practice's ACOG-derived clinical knowledge base, the patient education library, and the integrated MountZaraMedicalTranscription documentation pipeline are operational tools in service of this standard, not substitutes for clinical judgment.

## Section 3 — Telehealth-appropriate encounters

Telehealth is appropriate for: history-taking encounters where physical examination is not the primary diagnostic lever; review of imaging or laboratory results; medication management and refills (subject to the controlled-substances policy at `docs/compliance/controlled-substances.md`); post-operative follow-up; patient education; counseling and shared decision-making conversations. Telehealth is offered for these encounter types at the patient's preference.

## Section 4 — Telehealth-inappropriate encounters

Telehealth is not appropriate for: surgical decision-making encounters that require pelvic examination (the chaperone rule in the visit-type catalog at `functions/_lib/visit_types.js` governs the specific cases); any visit involving controlled-substance prescribing without prior in-person evaluation in the past 24 months (per the controlled-substances policy); office procedures (IUD placement, endometrial biopsy, colposcopy, hysteroscopy); any encounter where the patient has expressed a preference for in-person care; any encounter where the AI scheduling triage has recommended in-person and the clinician has agreed.

## Section 5 — Escalation criteria

During any telehealth encounter, the clinician will pause and route to in-person or emergency care if any of the following arises: acute hemodynamic instability; signs suggestive of an acute abdomen; new neurologic deficits; expressions of suicidal or homicidal ideation (Joshi & Welch 2023, p. 26 — telehealth-inappropriate); evidence of acute domestic violence in the patient's environment (p. 26); any clinical sign requiring in-person evaluation that the clinician identifies in real time. The clinician documents the escalation in the visit note.

## Section 6 — Documentation parity

Every encounter is documented at the same depth regardless of modality. The MountZaraMedicalTranscription pipeline generates the structured note; the clinician reviews and finalizes; the note enters the patient's chart. Telehealth encounters carry no documentation discount.

## Section 7 — Annual review

This statement is reviewed annually in January by Dr. Mabini, alongside the controlled-substances policy (R2) and the licensure policy (R3), with cross-reference to evolving telehealth standard-of-care case law. Revisions are dated; the prior version is retained.
