# Section 1 • Program Extension Rationale & Overview

## Program Description — 3-Year Extension

### Mission and Goals (Updated)
The Fellowship in Minimally Invasive Gynecologic Surgery (FMIGS) at Ascension Saint Francis Hospital has been extended from a two-year to a **three-year** training program to provide an enhanced, competency-based educational experience that further prepares gynecologic surgeons to become leaders in complex benign gynecologic surgery, surgical innovation, research, and community health advocacy. The additional year allows for deepened subspecialty exposure, expanded research productivity, increased operative autonomy, and leadership development in preparation for independent MIGS practice. The fellowship emphasizes equity-driven care, ensuring fellows develop expertise in addressing disparities in gynecologic healthcare while implementing evidence-based minimally invasive surgical techniques. Through multidisciplinary collaboration with urogynecology, colorectal, bariatric, and gynecologic expert physicians in vNOTES, fellows achieve clinical excellence in advanced MIGS techniques including laparoscopic hysterectomy, myomectomy, excision of deep infiltrating endometriosis, and robotic surgery.

### Comprehensive Training Environment
Fellows complete rotations across four major hospital systems serving diverse patient populations: Ascension Saint Francis Hospital (Evanston), Saint Joseph Hospital (Lincoln Park), Saint Mary Hospital (West Town), and Thorek Memorial Hospital (Uptown), ranging from affluent communities (median income $98,426) to underserved populations (22% below poverty line). This unique multi-site training provides exposure to complex case variety including high-prevalence conditions including fibroids in African American women, endometriosis management, and culturally sensitive care for immigrant populations. The program integrates structured didactic education, simulation-based training using AI-based models and 3D printing, and graduated responsibility advancement ensuring fellows develop both technical proficiency and cultural competency essential for addressing health disparities.

### Curriculum Structure and Innovation (3-Year Model)
The **three-year** curriculum combines rigorous clinical training with mandatory research components, requiring fellows to complete minimum case requirements per AAGL FMIGS Board while conducting research with national presentation and publication expectations. Year 3 consolidates and extends the competency-based training of Years 1 and 2, with emphasis on **independent surgical practice, teaching leadership, and transition to autonomous MIGS practice**. The program incorporates innovative educational strategies including AR surgical training, tele-mentoring, augmented reality platforms, and technology-enhanced learning through both on-site and other society online resources. Fellows engage in community outreach initiatives, health education advocacy, and quality improvement methodologies, preparing them to serve as clinical experts, educators, and leaders in academic medicine while advancing evidence-based practices in minimally invasive and complex gynecologic surgery.

### Rationale for Third Year Extension
1. **Enhanced Surgical Autonomy** — Additional operative volume and complexity to achieve full independent practice readiness
2. **Research Maturation** — Extended timeline for IRB-approved studies to reach completion, publication, and dissemination
3. **Teaching Leadership** — Opportunity to mentor junior trainees and lead resident didactics and simulation labs
4. **Faculty Transition** — Integration of new MIGS-trained faculty (Dr. Sankey-Thomas) to ensure continuity of subspecialty training
5. **Case Diversity** — Expanded exposure across all four hospital sites to maximize procedural range and patient population diversity

### Year 3 Rotation Design Philosophy

The Year 3 Multi-Focus Rotation Model builds upon the Year 2 structure with the following enhancements:

**Educational Framework**
- **Primary Focus:** Independent surgical leadership with attending available but supervision at a distance
- **Secondary Exposure:** Cross-subspecialty consultation role, functioning as MIGS consultant to general OB/GYN attendings
- **Site Optimization:** Multi-site rotations leverage each hospital's unique patient demographics and case mix with fellow driving case selection
- **Competency Progression:** Fellow functions at the level of near-independent practitioner across all subspecialties

**Case Volume Strategy**
- Distributing cases across 4 hospital systems with different patient populations
- Combining subspecialties that share common surgical approaches (e.g., MIGS + Urogynecology)
- Leveraging faculty expertise at each site for optimal learning opportunities
- Maintaining continuity of MIGS cases throughout all rotations as the core fellowship focus
- **Year 3 addition:** Fellow leads complex case planning, pre-operative workup, and surgical decision-making with attending confirmation

# Section 6 • Year 3 Graduated Responsibility Framework

## Documentation Describing Graduated Responsibility — Year 3 Extension

### Core Requirements
During the educational program, the fellow must be supervised in all clinical activities, including surgical procedures. The FMIGS Surgical Competency List must be used and completed for each fellow by the end of the fellowship training.

### Progressive Structure — 3-Year Model
The extended 3-year fellowship is structured to demonstrate a clear and measurable progression in clinical and teaching responsibilities across the full span of the program.

---

### Graduated Responsibility Framework — Complete 3-Year Progression

#### Year 1 — Foundation and Skill Development
*Building core competencies and progressive autonomy*

| Phase | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Observation and Assisted Performance** | Direct supervision with hands-on assistance in all procedures |
| **Clinical Focus** | Comprehensive advanced surgical procedures, robotic surgery and laparoscopy |
| **Competency Goals** | Demonstrate proficiency in general laparoscopy skills/techniques; basic robotic system direct supervision |
| **Outcome Measures** | Regular case presentations and technical skill assessments |

#### Year 2 — Advanced Practice and Leadership
*Developing leadership and independent practice capabilities*

| Phase | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Progressive Independence** | Continued guidance and supervision with graduated autonomy |
| **Clinical Focus** | Perform simple to complex surgeries with increased autonomy |
| **Teaching Integration** | Begin mentoring residents and provide feedback on their performance in case-based clinical reviews |
| **Assessment Framework** | Assessment of competency and teaching effectiveness indicators; multisource evaluation |

#### Year 3 — Mastery, Independent Practice, and Teaching Leadership
*Achieving autonomous practice and educational leadership*

| Phase | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Advanced Surgical Independence** | Supervision at a distance; fellow serves as primary surgeon for all case complexities |
| **Clinical Leadership** | Lead complex case planning, multidisciplinary conferences, and patient management decisions |
| **Consultant Role** | Function as MIGS consultant to general OB/GYN attendings and ED physicians; comprehensive operative assessment and management |
| **Educational Leadership** | Design and deliver resident didactic lectures; lead simulation lab sessions; mentor junior learners in the operating room |
| **Research Leadership** | Lead research projects to completion and publication; present at national conferences as primary author |
| **Quality Improvement** | Lead departmental QI initiatives; participate in M&M conferences as case presenter and discussion leader |
| **Transition to Practice** | Independent outpatient clinic management; pre-operative workup and surgical scheduling; post-operative care leadership |
| **Final Assessment** | Comprehensive summative evaluation of independent practice readiness; multisource assessment of surgical competency, teaching effectiveness, research productivity, and professionalism |

### Year 3 Milestone Progression

| Milestone Domain | Year 1 Target | Year 2 Target | **Year 3 Target** |
|-----------------|---------------|---------------|-------------------|
| Patient Care | Level 2–3 | Level 3–4 | **Level 4–5** |
| Medical Knowledge | Level 2–3 | Level 3–4 | **Level 4–5** |
| Practice-Based Learning | Level 2 | Level 3 | **Level 4–5** |
| Interpersonal Communication | Level 2–3 | Level 3–4 | **Level 4–5** |
