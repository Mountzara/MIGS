Section 1

Program Extension Rationale & Overview

Mission and Goals (Updated)

The Fellowship in Minimally Invasive Gynecologic Surgery (FMIGS) at Ascension Saint Francis Hospital has been extended from a two-year to a three-year training program to provide an enhanced, competency-based educational experience that further prepares gynecologic surgeons to become leaders in complex benign gynecologic surgery, surgical innovation, research, and community health advocacy.

The additional year allows for deepened subspecialty exposure, expanded research productivity, increased operative autonomy, and leadership development in preparation for independent MIGS practice. The fellowship emphasizes equity-driven care, ensuring fellows develop expertise in addressing disparities in gynecologic healthcare while implementing evidence-based minimally invasive surgical techniques.

Comprehensive Training Environment

Fellows complete rotations across four major hospital systems serving diverse patient populations: Ascension Saint Francis Hospital (Evanston), Saint Joseph Hospital (Lincoln Park), Saint Mary Hospital (West Town), and Thorek Memorial Hospital (Uptown), ranging from affluent communities (median income $98,426) to underserved populations (22% below poverty line).

Rationale for Third Year Extension

  • Enhanced Surgical Autonomy — Additional operative volume and complexity to achieve full independent practice readiness
  • Research Maturation — Extended timeline for IRB-approved studies to reach completion, publication, and dissemination
  • Teaching Leadership — Opportunity to mentor junior trainees and lead resident didactics and simulation labs
  • Faculty Transition — Integration of new MIGS-trained faculty (Dr. Sankey-Thomas) to ensure continuity of subspecialty training
  • Case Diversity — Expanded exposure across all four hospital sites to maximize procedural range and patient population diversity

Dr. Maria Teresa Tam (Primary), Dr. Sankey-Thomas (Secondary)

Objectives
