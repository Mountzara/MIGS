Chapter One — Origins

A purpose found in loss.

In 2005, Dr. Mabini's mother passed away from pancreatic cancer. He was a premed student in California, working through his bachelor's at UC Irvine, when he found himself unexpectedly at the helm of Nurses Plus Hospice — the organization she had founded after serving more than 20 years as an OBGYN nurse with Kaiser Permanente. Caring for women — through pregnancy, surgery, recovery, and everything in between — remained the work of her life to the very end, both professionally and personally.

He was twenty-something, with no formal management training, leading an end-of-life care company through a deeply personal grief. What carried him wasn't a résumé — it was what his mother had taught him in her final months: the quiet, unwavering sense of purpose that comes from caring for someone all the way to the end.

Within a few years, the company had earned Joint Commission accreditation — the gold standard of healthcare quality.

It was the first time Dr. Mabini learned that purpose, when paired with relentless work ethic, could turn impossible into inevitable. It would become the foundation of everything that followed.

Chapter Two — Heritage

Filipino-American roots.
A maker's hands.

Dr. Mabini's Filipino heritage isn't a footnote — it's a discipline. He cooks traditional dishes the way some surgeons review anatomy: with reverence, repetition, and an eye for what the small details reveal about the larger system.

Cooking, music production, and dance — earlier in his life — gave him something most surgeons don't bring to the OR: a creative instinct for problem-solving. The same hands that learned Best Hip Hop Dance Team choreography at UC Irvine became the hands that now navigate complex laparoscopic anatomy.

He competed for UC Irvine's Divisional Rugby Conference Champion team. He earned a Postgraduate Certificate in Public Health from the University of Liverpool. Each chapter widened the lens.

Chapter Three — The Path to Surgery

A doctor of the whole person.

Dr. Mabini chose Osteopathic Medicine deliberately — drawn to a tradition that treats the body as an integrated system, not a collection of organ silos. At the Alabama College of Osteopathic Medicine, he earned a competitive Anatomy and Osteopathic Principles & Practice Preclinical Fellowship, immersing himself in dissection-based teaching and the four core osteopathic manipulative techniques: Fascial Distortion Model, Muscle Energy, Counterstrain, and Myofascial Release.

His preclinical Anatomy & OPP Fellowship at ACOM was paired with a combined Master's in Adult Education through Troy University — concentration in curriculum development and instructional design. Built into the fellowship pathway, the MSAEd gave him formal training in how adult learners actually acquire complex skills, which most surgeons-in-training never receive. Dr. Mabini saw the connection clearly: great surgeons don't just operate. They teach and learn from their life experiences.

The decision to pursue OBGYN — and ultimately Complex Benign Gynecology and Minimally Invasive Gynecologic Surgery — was deliberate, and it was personal. It was the field his mother had given more than twenty years of her life to as a Kaiser Permanente OBGYN nurse, and the field she loved. Choosing it was a way to solidify his connection to her — to step into the same operating rooms, hallways, and patient stories she had spent her career inside, and to carry her work forward in the only language a son of an immigrant OB RN could speak.

Residency at Ascension Illinois St. Alexius followed. He emerged as Surgical Skills Chief — designing how the next generation would learn — and earned the Excellence in Minimally Invasive Gynecology recognition from AAGL.

Chapter Four — Innovation

Where women's health and surgery meet innovation.

At PRIME St. Francis Hospital in Evanston, Illinois, Dr. Mabini entered the Complex Benign Gynecology Fellowship determined to push the field, not just inhabit it.

His pioneering research with Indocyanine Green (ICG) imaging — using fluorescent dye in ways the field hadn't traditionally explored — earned the Dr. George W. Morley Memorial Paper Award at the CAOG 91st Annual Meeting in Indianapolis, October 2024. The work showed that ICG could illuminate endometriosis-related fibrosis and inflammatory changes invisible under standard white light. It had implications for how aggressively, and how completely, the disease could be excised.

The following month, his video abstract on a visually directed hysteroscopic approach to retained products of conception in an arcuate uterus — a case that classically defies blind dilation and curettage — won the AAGL Golden Hysteroscope Award at the 2024 Global Congress in New Orleans. Best Video Abstract in Hysteroscopy. National recognition. Raising the bar in surgical education.

Behind both awards: the same instinct from his mother's hospice. See clearly. Then do the work.

Chapter Five — Education

Building curriculums for the adult learner.

The Master's in Adult Education has never sat idle. Dr. Mabini designed the CBG/MIGS institutional curriculum at PRIME St. Francis — integrating AI and advanced technologies into how surgeons learn through surgery.

The premise is simple, and rare: experiential learning principles applied to surgical training. The next generation of CBG/MIGS surgeons should not have to assemble their education from disconnected lectures, textbooks, and YouTube. The curriculum he built is structured, evidence-based, and unapologetically modern.

For Dr. Mabini, the operating room and classroom aren't separate places. They are the same place at different scales.

Chapter Six — The Pulse of Progress

Research, every day.

Currently, Dr. Mabini leads multiple IRB-approved research initiatives: studies on Narrow-Band Imaging in adenomyosis detection during hysteroscopy, post-operative pain management following transversus abdominis plane (TAP) blocks in CBG/MIGS within ERAS protocols, and the detection of appendiceal endometriosis using ICG.

His publications appear in the Journal of Minimally Invasive Gynecology, Archives of Gynecology and Obstetrics, and Gynecology and Minimally Invasive Therapy. Topics range from mapping deep epigastric vessels by BMI to redefine laparoscopic safe zones, to the use of gel-based port systems in robotic myomectomy, to ICG-guided isthmocele repair in patients with Asherman's syndrome.

Research, for him, is never academic exercise. It is the pulse of progress in a field that, without curious surgeons, will never advance.

Chapter Seven — Vision

Where this is going.

Dr. Mabini is also a builder of software. The Mount Zara suite — six native applications spanning clinical AI, board prep, transcription, research workflow, surgical video archival, and DICOM imaging — is a working surgeon's answer to the question: what would the tools look like if we built them ourselves?

His skills are unusual on paper: surgeon, educator, AI/LLM developer, medical 3D printer, video producer. In practice, they form a single coherent stance — that women's health deserves modern tools, designed by the people who use them.

His days move between the OR at PRIME St. Francis, locum cases across Chicago and the country, the desk where the Mount Zara suite is built, and the classrooms and pages where the work gets shared. On the off days, he climbs with his dog, Marty. The roles look varied — the practice underneath is one.
