FIGO Types 0–8 — why location matters more than size

Fibroids vary enormously in size, number, and most importantly location. The FIGO system classifies them on an 8-step scale based on their relationship to the endometrial cavity and the uterine serosa[3]Munro et al., FIGO classification system (PALM-COEIN) for causes of abnormal uter..., Int J Gynaecol Obstet 2011 · PMID 21345435. Location predicts symptoms and treatment choice better than size does.

Submucosal (cavity-distorting)

  • Type 0 — entirely intracavitary on a stalk. Often a 20-minute hysteroscopic procedure.
  • Type 1 — mostly in the cavity, with <50% intramural extension.
  • Type 2 — mostly intramural with ≥50% extension, partially into the cavity. May require staged hysteroscopic procedures depending on size.

These bleed the most for their size. Often the cause when an otherwise small fibroid causes major HMB.

Intramural & subserosal

  • Type 3 — entirely intramural, contacting endometrium.
  • Type 4 — entirely intramural, not contacting endometrium or serosa.
  • Type 5 — subserosal with ≥50% intramural.
  • Type 6 — subserosal with <50% intramural.
  • Type 7 — pedunculated subserosal (on a stalk on the outside).
  • Type 8 — other locations (cervical, parasitic).

These cause bulk symptoms more than bleeding. Removed laparoscopically when uterus-preserving treatment is the goal.

What fibroids do — and the treatment options that match

The four symptom domains drive treatment choice:

1

Heavy menstrual bleeding

Especially with submucosal Types 0–2. Best treated by removing the cavity-distorting fibroid or by suppressing the bleeding medically.

2

Bulk & pressure symptoms

Urinary frequency, constipation, abdominal distension, lower-back ache. Reflects fibroid size, not type. Larger uteri get bulk symptoms regardless of FIGO type.

3

Pelvic pain

Dysmenorrhea, dyspareunia, or chronic pelvic pain. Often coexists with adenomyosis. Acute pain may signal fibroid degeneration.

4

Fertility concerns

Submucosal Types 0–1 strongly impair fertility and should be removed[7]Pritts et al., Fibroids and infertility: an updated systematic review of the evidence, Fertil Steril 2009 · PMID 18339376. Intramural >4 cm without cavity distortion may impair fertility — less clear evidence.

The treatment menu

M

Treatment with medication

Tranexamic acid, NSAIDs, hormonal contraceptives, LNG-IUD, GnRH antagonists with add-back therapy (elagolix)[5]Schlaff et al., Elagolix for Heavy Menstrual Bleeding in Women with Uterine Fibroids, N Engl J Med 2020 · PMID 31971678. First-line for HMB-dominant disease with no bulk symptoms.

H

Hysteroscopic myomectomy

Outpatient, no incisions. The right answer for symptomatic submucosal Types 0–2.

L

Laparoscopic myomectomy

For intramural and subserosal fibroids when the uterus is being preserved. Less blood loss + faster recovery than open.

U

Uterine artery embolization

Interventional radiology alternative to surgery. Reduces fibroid volume 40–60%[6]Goodwin et al., Uterine artery embolization for treatment of leiomyomata: long-term..., Obstet Gynecol 2008 · PMID 18165389. Not for women planning future pregnancy.

Most common patient questions

Do I have to do anything if I have a fibroid?

No. Asymptomatic fibroids are typically managed expectantly — periodic monitoring by exam and ultrasound, with treatment offered only when symptoms warrant it[1]?, Management of Symptomatic Uterine Leiomyomas: ACOG Practice Bulleti..., Obstet Gynecol 2021 · PMID 34011888. Many fibroids stay stable or even shrink after menopause. The decision to treat is driven by your symptoms, not by the fibroid’s presence on imaging.

Can fibroids turn into cancer?

Fibroids themselves do not become cancer — they are monoclonal benign tumors with a different cell of origin than leiomyosarcoma. The clinical question is whether what looks like a fibroid on imaging might actually be a leiomyosarcoma masquerading as a fibroid. Meta-analyses estimate this occult-sarcoma risk at ~1 per 1000 surgeries for presumed fibroids[1]?, Management of Symptomatic Uterine Leiomyomas: ACOG Practice Bulleti..., Obstet Gynecol 2021 · PMID 34011888. Rapid growth (especially in postmenopausal women), unusual imaging features, and certain demographic factors raise suspicion. No preoperative test reliably distinguishes the two, which informs how the specimen is removed (no power morcellation in suspicious cases).

I want to keep my uterus — what are my options?

Several. Treatment with medication (LNG-IUD, GnRH antagonists[5]Schlaff et al., Elagolix for Heavy Menstrual Bleeding in Women with Uterine Fibroids, N Engl J Med 2020 · PMID 31971678, tranexamic acid), hysteroscopic myomectomy for cavity-distorting fibroids, laparoscopic myomectomy for intramural/subserosal disease, and uterine artery embolization. Each preserves the uterus. The choice depends on fibroid type and location (FIGO classification), your symptoms, your fertility plans, and how soon you want symptom relief.

Can I get pregnant after a myomectomy?

Yes — restoring fertility is one of the main reasons to choose myomectomy over UAE or hysterectomy in a woman who wants future pregnancy. Hysteroscopic removal of submucosal fibroids restores cavity contour and pregnancy outcomes[7]Pritts et al., Fibroids and infertility: an updated systematic review of the evidence, Fertil Steril 2009 · PMID 18339376. After laparoscopic or open myomectomy for intramural fibroids, you can carry a pregnancy. Cesarean delivery is often recommended depending on how deep the myomectomy entered the muscle — that’s a per-case discussion based on operative findings.

Is UAE a good option for me?

UAE works well for women with symptomatic fibroids who want to preserve the uterus and are not planning future pregnancy — fibroid volume reduction of 40–60% and substantial symptom improvement, with shorter recovery than surgery[6]Goodwin et al., Uterine artery embolization for treatment of leiomyomata: long-term..., Obstet Gynecol 2008 · PMID 18165389. The trade-offs: ~20–30% need a subsequent hysterectomy over 5 years, ovarian reserve can be affected (which is why it’s avoided in women planning pregnancy), and rare complications include uterine necrosis. It’s a great fit for the right patient.

How big is “big”?

Size matters mainly for bulk symptoms, surgical technique, and whether a fibroid is easily removed hysteroscopically. There’s no single size threshold that mandates intervention — a 1 cm submucosal fibroid causing HMB and infertility may need removal, while a 7 cm asymptomatic intramural fibroid in a postmenopausal woman may need no treatment. Decisions are individualized.

Why are fibroids worse for Black women?

This is a real and documented disparity[2]Baird et al., High cumulative incidence of uterine leiomyoma in black and white w..., Am J Obstet Gynecol 2003 · PMID 12548202: Black women have 2–3 times the incidence of fibroids, earlier onset, larger fibroids, more severe symptoms, and historically have been offered hysterectomy at higher rates than other interventions. Both biological factors (vitamin D status, hormone metabolism) and structural factors (delayed diagnosis, access to subspecialty care) contribute. The practice prioritizes uterus-preserving treatment options whenever fertility or autonomy supports it.
