What it actually is

Tissue that belongs in one place, growing in another — and behaving badly.

Every month, the lining of your uterus (the endometrium) thickens and then sheds during your period. In endometriosis, tissue that looks and behaves like that lining ends up outside the uterus — on the ovaries, on the ligaments that hold the uterus in place, on the bladder, the bowel, the lining of the pelvis. Each month, that tissue still tries to bleed[1]Diagnosis of Endometriosis.Obstet Gynecol 2026 · PMID 41712950ACOG Clinical Practice Guideline — Diagnosis of Endometriosis. Affirms clinical diagnosis as sufficient: laparoscopy is no longer required to start treatment.. But it has nowhere to go.

That trapped bleeding causes inflammation, scarring (called adhesions), and over time, deeply sensitized pain pathways. Endometriosis is not a disease of unusually heavy or painful periods alone — it is an estrogen-driven, inflammatory condition that grows and changes over years if it is not treated[2]Laparoscopic surgery for endometriosis.Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2020 · PMID 33095458Cochrane systematic review — laparoscopic excision provides overall pain relief versus no treatment or diagnostic-only laparoscopy in endometriosis..

Why it hurts

Four overlapping pain sources, all happening at the same time.

One of the reasons endometriosis is so often under-treated is that pain in this condition is never coming from just one place. Treating only the lesions — with hormones or even with surgery — leaves the other three layers untreated, which is why so many women continue to hurt after “the surgery went well.” Click any card to read the deeper dive on that layer.

The osteopathic layer

OMT does not replace anything you are already doing. It addresses what they can’t.

This is a DO-led CBG/MIGS practice — a fully licensed gynecologic surgeon with additional training in osteopathic manipulative treatment. For pelvic pain, OMT addresses the structural and neurological contributors that hormones and surgery don’t directly reach, layered on top of standard care rather than instead of it.

From the clinic counseling script, verbatim:

In endometriosis, pain comes from at least four overlapping sources simultaneously: the lesions themselves releasing inflammatory chemicals like prostaglandins and cytokines; the nerves around the lesions, which become abnormally sensitive over time; the pelvic-floor muscles, which develop trigger points and tightness as a guarding response; and the fascia and ligaments — especially the broad ligament and uterosacral ligaments — which can become restricted by adhesions and inflammation. Hormonal therapy reduces the activity of the lesions but does not directly address the muscle tightness, the fascial restriction, or the sensitized pain pathways — which is why some women continue to have pain even after good hormonal control or after excision surgery.

What exactly is OMT, and how is it different from chiropractic care or massage?

OMT — osteopathic manipulative treatment — is performed by licensed physicians (DOs and MDs with additional training). It uses a range of hands-on techniques (spinal manipulation, visceral mobilization, fascial release, counterstrain, muscle energy) integrated into full medical care: the same physician can perform OMT, prescribe your medications, and operate on you when needed.

Chiropractic care focuses on spinal manipulation; chiropractors are not licensed to prescribe medications or perform medical procedures. Massage targets broad muscle relaxation but does not treat the spinal segmental, fascial, or visceral contributors that drive pelvic pain.

Will the OMT hurt?

Most techniques are gentle and should not be painful. High-velocity-low-amplitude (HVLA) thrust techniques produce a brief sensation — sometimes with an audible pop — but should not be sharp or painful. We escalate. The standard step-up: first-line OCP or NSAIDs → second-line progestin-only or LNG-IUD → third-line GnRH antagonist with add-back hormones. What’s the difference between excision and ablation?

Ablation burns the surface of the lesion away. Excision cuts the lesion out entirely, including the deeper tissue you can’t see from the surface. For ovarian endometriomas the published evidence is clear: excising the cyst wall (cystectomy) is superior to draining and ablating it — lower recurrence and better symptom relief[2]Laparoscopic surgery for endometriosis.Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2020 · PMID 33095458Cochrane systematic review — laparoscopic excision provides overall pain relief versus no treatment or diagnostic-only laparoscopy in endometriosis.. For peritoneal disease, excision is also preferred when technically feasible. Excision is performed laparoscopically as the default approach.

Skipping post-op suppression is the most common reason for symptomatic recurrence in the published series.

Is surgery safe?

Laparoscopic excision is generally low-risk when performed by a surgeon with subspecialty training in CBG/MIGS. I think the pain is in my head. Could that be it?

It is not “in your head,” but the nervous system is part of the story — in a real, physical way. Acknowledging the nervous-system component is not dismissing your pain. It is the opposite: it is taking it seriously enough to actually address it.

When to call urgently

Symptoms that should not wait for your next visit.

Call the office or seek care now if you have:
  • Sudden, severe one-sided pelvic pain — especially with nausea or vomiting (possible ovarian torsion)
  • Pain that prescribed medications are no longer touching
  • Bleeding heavy enough to soak through a pad or tampon every hour for two consecutive hours
  • Fever above 101°F with pelvic pain
  • New bowel symptoms — rectal bleeding, severe constipation, or pain with bowel movements that wasn’t there before
  • New urinary symptoms — blood in the urine, inability to fully empty the bladder
  • Pain so severe you cannot work, sleep, or function
  • If you suspect you are pregnant and you are on hormonal therapy
